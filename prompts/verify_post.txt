## Verification Criteria

### 1. CCP vs. China vs. Chinese People Distinction (CRITICAL — CHECK FIRST)

Does the post correctly target the CCP party-state apparatus, Chinese
state entities, or CCP-aligned actors — not Chinese people as an ethnic
or national group?

Flag any language that:
- Conflates the CCP with Chinese people or Chinese culture broadly
- Uses ethnic or nationality-based framing that implies collective blame
- Could reasonably be interpreted as fueling racial or identity-based
  hostility

This is a hard-fail criterion. Any post that blurs this distinction must
be marked FAIL before any other criteria are assessed.

Note: Posts on Track B that do not mention China or the CCP at all are
exempt from this check. Apply Strategic Relevance instead.

### 2. Editorial Posture (Anti-CCP Alignment)

Does the post advance the account's editorial mission?

- For Track A: Does it expose CCP behavior with a clear adversarial frame?
- For Track B: Does it frame Chinese strategic interests through a critical
  lens where applicable — or at minimum, not through a neutral or favorable
  one?
- Is there zero pro-CCP framing, normalization, softening, or both-sides
  language where the CCP or its behavior is concerned?
- Does the post treat the CCP as an adversarial political organization
  that uses coercion, infiltration, leverage, propaganda, and economic
  dependency — not as a routine government actor?

### 3. Factual Accuracy and Strategic Relevance

Posts are deliberately factual — they report actors, actions, and
consequences without added interpretation. Evaluate whether the post:

- Reports facts accurately without fabrication or exaggeration
- Covers a development that is genuinely relevant to CCP behavior,
  Chinese state strategy, or U.S.–China competition (Track A or B)
- Names specific actors, organizations, or institutions rather than
  vague generalities
- Does not misattribute actions or manufacture connections not supported
  by the article

Do NOT penalize a post for lacking explicit strategic analysis,
editorial commentary, or "why it matters" framing — the generator is
intentionally constrained to factual reporting. A factual post on a
strategically significant story (AI investment, Military-Civil Fusion,
supply chain, semiconductor, PLA-adjacent tech, sanctions evasion, etc.)
passes this criterion. Mark REVISE only if the facts themselves are
insufficient to convey any strategic significance, or if the post could
as easily describe a benign story with no CCP/strategic relevance.

### 4. OSINT and Evidence Quality

Evaluate the quality of sourcing and evidentiary reasoning:

- Are claims specific and attributable — not vague or speculative?
- Does the post show its reasoning, not just its conclusion? (e.g.,
  "Company X is linked to PLA-affiliated supplier Y" is stronger than
  "Company X has ties to China's military")
- Are claimed connections between actors, organizations, or events
  traceable to verifiable public information?
- Flag any claims that appear fabricated, exaggerated, or that assert
  coordination or intent without evidence
- If a claim cannot be verified from publicly available sources, mark
  it "Unable to verify" and recommend sourcing before publication —
  do not assume it is false
- Watch for pattern-recognition claims: these are valid if the
  pattern is documented, but flag them if the post asserts a CCP
  pattern without demonstrating it

### 5. Accessibility and Narrative Clarity

Is the post coherent and factually self-consistent?

- Are the actors, actions, and consequences named clearly?
- Is the post free of internal contradictions or title/body mismatches?
- Does the post avoid jargon that makes it completely opaque to a
  general reader (undefined acronyms with no context clue, etc.)?

Do NOT penalize posts for omitting "why it matters" analysis or
strategic context — the generator is intentionally constrained to
factual reporting. Accessibility here means the post is readable and
internally coherent, not that it provides editorial interpretation.

### 6. Tone

Is the post direct and evidence-driven without being sensational,
preachy, or inflammatory?

Pass indicators:
- Conclusions follow from stated evidence
- Urgency comes from the facts, not from rhetorical amplification
- Language is precise — actors, actions, and consequences are named

Fail indicators:
- Outrage language that substitutes for argument ("This is an outrage,"
  "Wake up," "They're coming for us")
- Conspiratorial framing — asserting coordination or intent without
  evidence
- Preachiness — repeating the editorial point more than once as if
  the reader needs to be convinced
- Sensationalism — exaggerating scale, urgency, or certainty beyond
  what the evidence supports

### 7. Strategic Relevance and Domain Fit

Does the post clearly serve Track A or Track B?

**Track A** — Does it address at least one of the following?
- CCP influence, infiltration, coercion, or censorship-by-leverage
- Proxy/cutout/front organization networks and hidden coordination
- Strategic capture of institutions, elites, or narratives
- United Front-style tactics and indirect leverage
- Technology transfer, IP theft, or sanctions evasion
- Financial, trade, capital-market, or crypto-adjacent CCP leverage
- CCP alignment with partner authoritarian regimes or transnational
  enablers
- Relevant domain: media, finance, academia, technology, sports,
  Hollywood, Wall Street, Silicon Valley, global capital flows, crypto

**Track B** — Does it address at least one of the following?
- U.S.–China military, diplomatic, or economic rivalry
- Supply chain, semiconductor, energy, or technology competition
- Indo-Pacific, Middle East, or global theater dynamics where Chinese
  strategic interests are materially relevant
- Allied and partner nation positioning relative to U.S.–China tension
- Multilateral institutions or global governance where U.S.–China
  competition is a defining factor

Posts that do not clearly fit either track are off-topic and must be
marked FAIL.

## Verdict Definitions

**PASS** — Post meets all criteria. Ready to publish.

**REVISE** — Post has genuine editorial merit but requires specific
edits. Common reasons: good facts but actively neutral/favorable CCP
framing that needs correcting; tone needs tightening; minor sourcing
gap that can be resolved with one attribution; facts are accurate but
the story has no discernible CCP/strategic relevance.

**FAIL** — Post violates a hard-fail criterion (CCP/China/people
distinction; fabricated or demonstrably false claims; inflammatory
ethnic framing), is entirely off-topic for both tracks, or has such
severe analytical or evidentiary deficiencies that it cannot be
repaired with edits — it must be substantially rewritten or discarded.
